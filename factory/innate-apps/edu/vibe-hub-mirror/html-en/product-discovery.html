Product Discovery

You might say

Don't have AI start coding features yet. Let's do product discovery first to see whether users actually care about this problem.

The exploration process of collecting user and business evidence to validate whether a problem is real, valuable, and feasible before committing to full development.·Suppose a team wants to build a prompt-result comparison tool. Discovery turns 'creators need comparisons' into a testable assumption, then gathers evidence from recent real stories and a low-fidelity task. The evidence may support continuing, narrowing, changing direction, or stopping. Discovery reduces early uncertainty; it does not guarantee product success.

How product discovery validates hypotheses before writing code
Example research recordPrompt-result comparison tool

There is an idea, but no user evidence yet

Value
Creators frequently need and will use result comparison
Usability
Beginners can understand two-column differences and choose
Feasibility
The same input can produce comparable results reliably

“I think users will like it” does not belong in the evidence column.

Discovery produces an evidence-backed next step, not automatic approval.

How a product idea becomes a testable decision

Before: the team treats a guess as user demand: The team assumes creators need prompt-result comparison and immediately asks AI to build accounts, dashboards, and settings. Shipping those features proves delivery, not that target users face the problem or would replace their current workaround.

Write risky assumptions, then gather behavioral evidence: Separate value, usability, feasibility, and business constraints. For example: creators manually compare results and this repeatedly causes rework. Reconstruct the last real event and workaround, then observe one selection task with a paper or low-fidelity prototype. Each assumption needs observable behavior; compliments alone are insufficient.

The outcome is a changed decision: If evidence supports only quick visual comparison, narrow the concept to a two-column view instead of expanding a full workspace. If the problem does not appear, pause and reassess. Continue, narrow, redirect, or stop are all valid outcomes when the evidence explains the choice.

Discovery is not endless meetings

Product discovery is not a meeting and has no universal sample size or method. Limited evidence only reduces uncertainty around the current assumption; new user, technical, or business evidence can reopen the decision.

Quick checkChoose the best answer

Research shows users manually compare results side by side, but they currently care only about spotting differences; nobody uses collaboration or multiple exports. What should happen next?
