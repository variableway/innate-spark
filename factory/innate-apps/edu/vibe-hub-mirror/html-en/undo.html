Undo

You might say

This change is wrong—undo it and put things back the way they were before I touched them.

Undo rolls your last action back exactly as it was—Ctrl+Z brings the wrongly deleted row back, no retyping.·Press Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac) to step backward, and redo to step forward again if you go too far. It has limits: usually only the last several steps, and the history is gone after refresh or close—important deletions still deserve a confirmation rather than relying on undo.

What undo remembers

The state before the action: Each step stores a "before" snapshot; pressing undo restores that original rather than regenerating it.

Paired with redo: Undo steps back, redo (Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y) re-applies the undone step. Both share one history.

History is finite: Usually only recent steps survive, and refresh or close clears it. Critical actions still deserve confirmation first.

What it is not

Undo is not "recreate": it restores the original record, ID and timestamps included, while recreating makes a new one. It also differs from rollback, which reverts a whole version such as a deployment. The same applies to working with AI—"undo this step" beats "make a new one" on both speed and safety.
